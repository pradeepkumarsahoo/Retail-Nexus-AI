Retail Nexus AI · Enterprise Agentic Retail Intelligence

Retail Nexus AI — an enterprise Agentic AI platform for retail intelligence.

Built for retailers, wholesalers and distribution operators. The platform ingests product, store, supplier, inventory and demand data from across the retail ecosystem — then detects exceptions, investigates them with specialised agents, predicts stockouts and supplier delays, simulates corrective actions, and routes consequential decisions through human approval. Every recommendation is evidence-backed and reversible.

01Problem statement

A large retail operator loses the equivalent of a mid-cap company's annual revenue every year to five preventable supply-chain failures. The pain is well-measured in industry research — and almost none of it is addressed by today's reporting stack.

What is broken today

Modern retail BI shows what already happened — yesterday's stockouts, last week's late trucks, last month's lost sales. The operator still cannot answer the actionable, forward-looking questions that move revenue:

  • Which products at which stores will stock out in the next 7 / 14 / 30 days?
  • Which suppliers are statistically likely to deliver late — and which open POs are affected?
  • Which demand swings are real spikes to protect against vs. promotion aftershocks to ignore?
  • For a given exception, what is the best intervention, and what is its net benefit in dollars?
  • Who authorised the decision, on what evidence, under which assumptions?

The gap. Dashboards describe the past; planners manually translate them into actions through spreadsheets and email. At 100 k daily exceptions on a 5 k-store chain, that translation layer is the constraint — not detection.

What it costs a $50 B retailer

Applying the industry benchmarks above to a single Tier-1 operator at the scale in §11 (10 k suppliers · 5 k stores · 100 k customers/day):

  • Stockouts — ~3 % of revenue leaks as lost sales ≈ $1.5 B / yr, plus unmeasured brand damage.
  • Late deliveries — ad-hoc expedites, penalties and cascade ripples ≈ $150 – 300 M / yr.
  • Excess & aging inventory — $5 – 8 B of working capital tied up with its 25 % annual holding cost ≈ $1.25 – 2 B / yr.
  • Slow decisions — detection-to-decision measured in days instead of minutes; the window to transfer, expedite or re-source closes before anyone acts.
  • Opaque recommendations — operators cannot approve what they cannot audit; AI pilots stall at the trust boundary, not the modelling one.

Headline. ~$3 – 4 B / yr of preventable supply-chain loss sits inside one large retailer — 6 – 8 % of revenue. Every lever is measurable, which means every lever is addressable.

02Solution

Retail Nexus AI — an enterprise Agentic AI platform that turns raw retail operational data into evidence-backed, approvable actions.

ADetect proactively

A unified exception engine sweeps across 11 categories (stockout risk, low DoS, supplier delay, demand spike / decline, excess, aging, replenishment mismatch, regional disruption, …) with a transparent 5-factor risk score.

BPredict with calibrated ML

Four separate predictive models — demand forecast, stockout, demand anomaly, supplier delay — each with an honest baseline (seasonal naive / 1-OTD / days-of-supply) before any gradient-boosted upgrade.

CInvestigate with agents

Six specialists + supervisor on a typed state machine: Demand, Inventory, Supplier Risk, Alternative Sourcing, Business Impact, Decision Orchestrator. Deterministic engines compute every number; LLMs only explain.

DSimulate interventions

A deterministic what-if engine compares baseline vs expedite / transfer / switch-supplier / increase-replenishment under explicit formulas and labelled assumptions.

EGovern consequential actions

Role-based approval tiered by risk. Approve · Reject · Request-more-evidence with full audit trail. Agents never write to real enterprise systems — execution is a simulated adapter.

FOperate reliably

Outbox event bus (→ SQS / SNS / EventBridge), structured JSON logs, OTEL traces, Prometheus metrics, per-run agent tracing, token & cost accounting.

GDefend every agent

OWASP Top 7 for LLM applications baked into the agent runtime: prompt-injection scan, PII & secret redaction, provider+model pinning, bound-checked evidence, JSON output validation, per-agent tool scope, system-prompt leakage scrub.

Why this architecture

  • Pure BI misses the decision. It shows numbers, not actions. The deliverable here is a schema-complete, evidence-anchored recommendation with net-benefit math attached.
  • Pure LLM hallucinates. A retail controller cannot approve a $-figure a model invented. Predictive models and deterministic rules produce every number; the LLM composes rationale over them.
  • Pure rules can't reason. The LLM tier interprets correlated signals across demand + supplier + inventory + promotions and writes the rationale a human reviewer needs in plain English.
  • Autonomous agents are unsafe. Every consequential action routes through role-gated human approval with a full audit trail — the only posture a retail CFO will sign off on.

Revenue impact preview

At the scale in §11 (10 k suppliers · 5 k stores · 100 k customers/day) and a run cost of $660 K / yr:

  • Internal deployment — recovers $425 – 720 M / yr per Tier-1 retailer. ROI 600 – 1,000 ×, payback under one week.
  • SaaS productisation — credible path to $75 – 150 M year-5 ARR across 50 – 110 retail logos in three pricing tiers.
  • Unit economics — $0.018 cost per decision vs $3 – 6 of margin on one avoided stockout: the platform sits on the right side of the economics curve.
  • Pilot framing — every recommendation already carries revenue-at-risk and net benefit, so a 90-day pilot produces its own ROI number instead of relying on these ranges.

06Agent workflow

Supervisor fans out to the subset of specialists the exception category requires. Each specialist is wrapped by a ThreatGuard that runs OWASP Top 7 pre- and post-flight checks (see §07). Max-step, retry and timeout are enforced per node.

07Security · OWASP Top 7 for LLM applications

A defensive layer wired into every specialist agent. Implemented in app/security/threat_model.py and invoked by each agent in app/agents/specialists.py. Findings are attached to AgentState.security_findings and the per-step trace for full audit.

Why I built it this way

Defence in depth, not replacement

The existing sanitize.validate_tool_call stays as the first ring. ThreatGuard adds the LLM-specific layer on top: injection, PII, bounds, output shape, agency, prompt-leak. Removing the guard still leaves the deterministic allow-list intact.

Soft-by-default, strict-on-demand

In soft mode the agent still produces evidence — findings travel in security_findings for observability. Strict mode (production profile) promotes any high-severity finding to a hard ThreatViolation so no poisoned number ever reaches the decision layer.

Auditable, not invisible

Every finding carries code · severity · message · detail · detected_at. They land on AgentState.security_findings and the step's outputs, so the operator sees them in the same trace view as token counts and rationale.

Narrow blast radius

Per-agent tool scope means a prompt-injection that successfully pivots inside one agent still cannot call tools the agent was never meant to call. The guard turns one layer of defence into seven, each failing loudly.

08Trade-off decisions — engineering judgement

These are my explicit engineering calls for this large-scale retail platform. Each decision names the alternative it rejects, the why, and the trade-off I accept. Signed off by Pradeep Sahoo as the responsible engineer.

LangGraph-equivalent orchestrator in pure Python rejected · add the langgraph + LangChain dependency chain
Why. The three features I actually need — typed state, conditional edges, approval interrupt — can be implemented in ~200 lines with full determinism and zero framework version risk. The spec itself discourages framework proliferation.
Trade-off accepted. No built-in checkpoint persistence or streaming; I'd migrate to langgraph if those become load-bearing. State is already fully typed, so the port is mechanical.
Deterministic math; LLM only explains rejected · let the LLM propose numeric fields
Why. A retail controller cannot approve a $-figure that an LLM invented. Predictive models and deterministic rules produce every number; the LLM composes the rationale and reports a bounded confidence.
Trade-off accepted. Less "chattiness". For conversational drill-down we'd add a dedicated QA endpoint later — I deliberately kept it out of the decision path.
Four separate predictive models rejected · one unified multi-task model
Why. Forecast / classification / anomaly / PO-level risk use different features, horizons and metric families. Each one carries its own baseline (seasonal naive / 1-OTD / DoS-logistic) so every upgrade is measured against something honest.
Trade-off accepted. More modules to maintain; clarity and auditability win over code volume at this scale.
Postgres + outbox pattern as the event source rejected · Kafka / Kinesis locally
Why. Business state change and its outbox row commit in the same DB transaction — no dual-write risk. Idempotent consumers keyed by event_id. Maps cleanly to SNS → SQS fan-out on AWS; no new infra for local runs.
Trade-off accepted. Single-node throughput ceiling. The ADR documents the triggers for switching to a distributed broker (cross-service fan-out, back-pressure, DLQ semantics).
Python / FastAPI as the primary API rejected · build the primary API in C# / .NET Core
Why. The ML and agent code is Python-native. Running two parallel API stacks over the same business logic doubles surface area and forces cross-process calls for the ML/agent path — all cost, no capability. OpenAPI 3.1 is the contract for any future .NET port.
Trade-off accepted. No .NET story in this build; the OpenAPI schema is auto-generable into controllers with nswag / openapi-generator when needed.
Postgres primary, SQLite in dev rejected · require Postgres for every local run
Why. Instant local loop for every engineer. SQLAlchemy dialect translation covers the entire schema. CI runs Postgres.
Trade-off accepted. SQLite's lighter FK enforcement — not a correctness issue for anything in the dev loop.
Simulated action adapter (no ERP write) rejected · let approved recommendations auto-execute
Why. The correct safety posture for an AI system proposing supply-chain actions. An approved recommendation emits ActionExecuted; a real ERP connector is a later integration, deliberately isolated from the agent code.
Trade-off accepted. No end-to-end "did it actually happen" loop in this build; that integration is scoped as future work with a well-defined contract.
Mock LLM default; Bedrock / OpenAI behind one interface rejected · require a paid provider to run the stack
Why. Reproducible, free, deterministic demos and tests. The provider abstraction is one interface — swap via LLM_PROVIDER env var.
Trade-off accepted. Live-provider latency / cost / schema compliance aren't measured by default; the harness supports it once a key is present.

Evaluation harness & release gate

AOffline eval

Golden dataset + metric suite runs on every PR. Required green before merge. Lives in backend/tests/eval/.

BShadow mode

New model runs alongside production for ≥ 7 days; outputs logged but not acted on. Operators periodically compare.

CCanary rollout

5 % → 25 % → 100 % traffic over 48 h. Auto-rollback on SLO burn or finding-severity spike.

DModel registry

Every model version is immutable, signed, and tied to the training commit hash + dataset hash. Rollback is a pointer change, not a redeploy.

Disaster recovery & rollback

ARTO / RPO

RTO ≤ 30 min (cross-region, warm-standby) · RPO ≤ 5 min (continuous RDS PITR + S3 cross-region replication).

BAgent & prompt versioning

Agent prompts, tool schemas, and the OWASP guard config are all versioned in-repo. Any of the three can be rolled back independently via a config flag — no redeploy.

CModel version rollback

Pointer swap in the model registry. Previous version always kept warm for 30 days. Rollback is tested quarterly as part of the DR drill.

DSchema evolution

DB migrations use expand-contract with a dual-read period. Breaking API changes require a 90-day deprecation window; /api/v2 ships alongside /api/v1 until clients cut over.

EChaos drills

Monthly game-day: kill a worker, choke SQS, inject 5xx on LLM, fail an AZ. Each drill ends with an SLO burn chart and a Fix-it ticket if the recovery procedure drifted.

FAudit & replay

Every decision is replay-safe: the raw feature frame, model version, prompt version and evidence snapshot are persisted together, so any past recommendation can be regenerated byte-for-byte.

18Phased transformation roadmap

Prototype → enterprise adoption in five stages. Each phase has a scope, a stage gate, and a measurable success criterion that must be green before the next phase starts. This is the shape of the conversation a CTO wants to have — not a Gantt chart, a value-and-risk curve.

Phase 1 · 0 – 3 mo
Data foundation & visibility

Land source feeds into the warehouse; stand up the feature store; publish lineage; ship the exception dashboard in read-only mode.

  • Gate — 95 % DQ pass on top 10 features, lineage queryable end-to-end.
  • Measure — detection latency ≤ 1 h.
Phase 2 · 3 – 6 mo
Predictive intelligence

Ship the four predictive models with the evaluation harness (§14). Shadow mode first, then operator-visible scores alongside the raw exception.

  • Gate — all four models beat their baseline on the §14 release gate.
  • Measure — stockout recall ≥ 0.70 on labelled history.
Phase 3 · 6 – 12 mo
Agent-assisted decisions

Turn on specialist agents with OWASP guard (§07); approval queue goes live; action adapter stays simulated. HITL on every Medium+ recommendation.

  • Gate — recommendation acceptance ≥ 60 % on Medium tier.
  • Measure — stockout rate ↓ 10 % vs baseline in pilot stores.
Phase 4 · 12 – 18 mo
Governed enterprise integrations

Wire real ERP / WMS / supplier EDI through a hardened adapter. Multi-tenant isolation hardened. SOC 2 Type II in progress.

  • Gate — tenant isolation + attestation evidence complete.
  • Measure — $425 M lower bound of §12 value validated in-flight.
Phase 5 · 18 – 30 mo
Multi-region scale & continuous optimisation

Active-active across two regions; continuous retraining; outcome-based pricing for SaaS tenants; chaos drills quarterly.

  • Gate — DR drill passes within RTO / RPO, two consecutive quarters.
  • Measure — 99.9 % availability over a rolling 90 days; SaaS logo count on plan.
